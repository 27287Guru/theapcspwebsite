SUBJECT
NATIONAL BROADCASTING COMPANY

In 1926, the National Broadcasting Company began building a new kind of shared experience: a radio network capable of bringing news, entertainment, music, and major events to listeners across the country.

Radio was more than a device in the living room. It was a connection to events happening hundreds or thousands of miles away. Families could listen to the same broadcasts and become part of a national audience.

As technology developed, NBC expanded into television, helping transform how Americans watched news, sports, comedy, and live events.

THE YEARS

SELECTED
HISTORICAL MILESTONES
1926

The first transmission

NBC is founded and launches its first nationwide radio broadcast on November 15. The network begins connecting listeners through shared programming and major events.

RADIO / ESTABLISHMENT
1929

Three notes become a signature

NBC introduces its famous three-note chimes as a recognizable network sound. A small audio cue becomes part of the identity of American broadcasting.

SOUND / BRAND IDENTITY
1939

The television experiment

NBC showcases television at the New York World's Fair. The technology points toward a future in which moving pictures can reach audiences in their own homes.

TELEVISION / EARLY BROADCAST
1947

Television finds an audience

NBC introduces programs including Howdy Doody and Kraft Television Theatre, helping establish television as a new destination for entertainment.

PROGRAMMING / LIVE TELEVISION
1952

Morning television arrives

TODAY launches as an early network morning show, combining news, interviews, and entertainment in a format that would become part of daily life.

NEWS / MORNING BROADCAST
1956

The peacock takes flight

NBC introduces its colorful peacock logo to promote color television. The design becomes one of the network's most recognizable symbols.

COLOR / NETWORK IDENTITY
1960

Politics enters the living room

NBC participates in the first televised presidential debate between John F. Kennedy and Richard Nixon, illustrating television's growing role in public life.

NEWS / NATIONAL EVENTS
1975

Saturday night changes

Saturday Night Live premieres, becoming a major part of NBC's comedy legacy and a long-running influence on American television culture.

COMEDY / ENTERTAINMENT
2004

A new media landscape

NBC and Universal merge to form NBCUniversal. Television now exists alongside cable, online media, and an increasingly connected digital world.

CORPORATE / DIGITAL ERA
2011

A new owner

Comcast completes its acquisition of NBCUniversal, bringing the company into a new stage of media distribution and technological development.

03 / THE TECHNOLOGY

FROM STATIC
TO STREAMING.

The history of NBC is also the history of how people receive information. Radio made live national broadcasts possible. Television added pictures. Color transformed the screen. Cable and digital media multiplied the choices available to audiences.

By the 2000s, the old idea of a single shared broadcast was changing. People could watch, record, replay, and eventually stream content in new ways.

The equipment changed. The screens changed. But the central idea remained: bringing stories and events to an audience.
